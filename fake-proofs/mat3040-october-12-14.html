MAT3040 Advanced Linear Algebra · October 12 / October 14

Statement

Let V be a finite-dimensional vector space and let S, T ⊆ V. If

Ann(S) = Ann(T),

then S = T.

Proposed proof

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that S ≠ T. Without loss of generality, choose

v ∈ S ∖ T.

Take a basis {v1, …, vk} of Span(T). Since v ∉ T, we may extend to a linearly independent set {v1, …, vk, v}.

By basis extension, extend it to a basis {v1, …, vk, v, vk+2, …, vn} of V.

Define f ∈ V* on this basis by

f(v1) = ··· = f(vk) = 0,    f(v) = 1,

and

f(vk+2) = ··· = f(vn) = 0.

Then f vanishes on Span(T), and hence f ∈ Ann(T).

By hypothesis Ann(T) = Ann(S), so f ∈ Ann(S). Since v ∈ S, this implies f(v) = 0, contradicting f(v) = 1. Therefore S = T. □
